# CAP Relevance Mapping: US NCII Legislation (TAKE IT DOWN Act)

## Overview

The **TAKE IT DOWN Act** (S. 146, 119th Congress; signed 19 May 2025) addresses the
nonconsensual publication of intimate visual depictions, including computer-generated
"digital forgeries". This document identifies CAP/SRP records that may be relevant to
it.

This is a relevance mapping, not a compliance mapping. CAP makes recorded generation
requests and refusal decisions auditable and attributable after the fact. It does not
block, filter or remove content; those actions are performed by the provider or
platform (VAP v1.2 §1.6 and Declaration).

> **Revision — 25 October 2026.** This page previously cited the unenacted
> 118th-Congress bill (S. 4569), listed section numbers and a civil remedy that do not
> match the enacted Act, equated the Act's 48-hour removal duty with generation-refusal
> latency, and described the SRP invariant as proof that content was never created. It
> has been corrected; see the [Git history](https://github.com/veritaschain/cap-spec/commits/main/docs/Regulatory-Mapping/US-NCII.md) for the earlier text.

## TAKE IT DOWN Act Summary

| Element | Content | CAP relevance |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| Criminal prohibitions | Amends 47 U.S.C. § 223 to prohibit knowingly publishing intimate visual depictions, including digital forgeries, of identifiable individuals without consent, and threats to do so | CAP does not record publication on third-party platforms. Where a generation service is involved, GEN_ATTEMPT / GEN / GEN_DENY records may be relevant evidence |
| Notice-and-removal | Covered platforms must provide a process for removal requests and remove a depiction no later than 48 hours after a valid request, making reasonable efforts to remove known identical copies; platforms had until 19 May 2026 to establish the process | CAP v1.0 defines no removal-request or removal event. A platform's removal timeline would need its own records |
| Enforcement | The Federal Trade Commission enforces the notice-and-removal requirements | — |

The Act is summarized here from the Congressional Research Service's legal sidebar
(LSB11314). It does not itself create a private civil right of action; a separate
federal civil action exists under 15 U.S.C. § 6851 (enacted in 2022).

## What SRP Records for NCII-Related Requests

| Evidentiary question | SRP record | Limit |
|----------------------|------------|-------|
| Was a recorded NCII-related request refused? | GEN_DENY with `RiskCategory: NCII_RISK` | Records the provider's decision; does not establish that the decision was correct |
| When was the recorded request refused? | `Timestamp` on GEN_ATTEMPT and GEN_DENY | Timing evidence for the generation decision, not for any platform removal |
| Does every recorded attempt have a recorded outcome? | SRP attempt–outcome invariant (CAP v1.0 §13) | Covers recorded attempts only. A request that was never recorded leaves nothing to detect (pre-measurement drop, VAP v1.2 Appendix A). The invariant cannot show that content was never generated by an unrecorded path |
| Have the records been altered? | Hash chain, signatures and external anchoring | Tamper-evidence, given trusted keys and anchors |

### Risk categories

```json
{
  "RiskCategory": "NCII_RISK",
  "RiskSubCategories": [
    "UPLOADED_PERSON",
    "CLOTHING_REMOVAL",
    "NON_CONSENSUAL_INTIMATE"
  ]
}
```

| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| NCII_RISK | Non-consensual intimate imagery |
| MINOR_SEXUALIZATION | Sexual content involving minors |
| REAL_PERSON_DEEPFAKE | Unauthorized real-person imagery |

## Evidence Pack

An Evidence Pack (CAP v1.0 §15) can contain the recorded GEN_ATTEMPT and GEN_DENY events,
anchors and verification material for a dispute. Sharing it with an affected person or
an authority is a disclosure decision for the provider; Evidence Packs contain hashes,
not raw prompts or images.

What an Evidence Pack can show:
1. A request was recorded (GEN_ATTEMPT)
2. The risk category assigned (RiskCategory)
3. The recorded decision (GEN_DENY)
4. The recorded times (Timestamps)
5. Whether the records verify against their hashes, signatures and anchors

What it cannot show: that no other request was made outside the recorded path, or that
content was not produced by another system.

## Investigation Support

| Investigation need | CAP record |
|--------------------|------------|
| Request timing | `Timestamp` |
| Request matching | `PromptHash` (pseudonymous; guessable for short inputs — see [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md)) |
| Actor identification | `ActorHash` (resolution requires the provider's records and legal process) |
| Model version | `ModelContext` |

Chain of custody:
1. Events are signed and hash-chained when created
2. External anchoring provides an independent timestamp (Silver and Gold; optional at Bronze in CAP v1.0)
3. The Evidence Pack is exported with verification instructions
4. A third party can verify the records independently of the producer

## Child Safety

CSAM_RISK and MINOR_SEXUALIZATION categories let refusal decisions involving minors be
recorded and audited. CAP does not detect, block or report CSAM; detection, refusal and
any mandatory reporting are the provider's obligations.

```json
{
  "EventType": "GEN_DENY",
  "RiskCategory": "CSAM_RISK",
  "RiskScore": 0.99,
  "ModelDecision": "DENY",
  "HumanOverride": false
}
```

---

## Implementation Notes

1. Record GEN_ATTEMPT before evaluation, so that each recorded attempt can be checked for a recorded outcome
2. Use the NCII_RISK, MINOR_SEXUALIZATION and CSAM_RISK categories consistently
3. Use Silver or Gold conformance (external anchoring daily or hourly)
4. Keep platform removal-request handling in separate records; CAP v1.0 does not cover it

## State Law Considerations

Many US states have their own NCII or synthetic-media laws, for example:

| State | Law |
|-------|-----|
| California | Civil Code §1708.86 |
| New York | Penal Law §245.15 |
| Texas | Penal Code §21.165 |
| Florida | Fla. Stat. §784.049 |

CAP records may serve as evidence in more than one jurisdiction. Whether they are
admissible or sufficient is for the competent court or authority.
